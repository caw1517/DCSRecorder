Visible from the start. Parked at the finish.

Explore the proposed lifecycle for one playback aircraft: inspect its recorded starting state, release motion and supported state together, and retain a completed hot parking endpoint. This is a model to review, not a DCS simulation or proof of ground stability.

Existing requirements: original recorded coordinates; player at the authored start; look around while held; no acquisition blend; complete supported initial state. Startup, shutdown, layers and full cockpit restoration are outside this decision.

The user approved this lifecycle and its bounded experiments: real-aircraft staging first, a proven stand-in only if necessary, a running surrounding mission, shared release after the countdown, hot parked completion, removal at valid non-parking endings, failure cleanup and full mission restart. Alternatives remain explorable for comparison. Changing a choice resets this demonstration. Buttons advance illustrative time, not real seconds.

Bounded simulator experiments proposed for implementation

Use the existing documented Hornet / Blue Angels, Caucasus, zero-wind and DCS-build baseline. Keep original recordings and mission revisions. These experiments are not run by this page.

ExperimentInspect and retainDecision gate
1 · Real-object held stagingOne stationary hot ground take and one airborne take. Inspect first visible frame, a 30-second hold and the full three-second countdown. Record pose, velocity, supported exterior/engine state, contact behavior, playback time, object identity/count, video and audio. Look around from the player's held position; observe a scene vehicle and timed script.Exact initial snapshot persists with replay time zero; scene clock matches the chosen policy. Active Pause alone is not assumed to hold all aircraft or state. Moving-start velocity must be restored at release without moving the held pose beforehand.
2 · Release and first movementReadiness failure and normal release for both takes. Capture release acknowledgment, first presentation frame and first taxi motion at the same sampled replay time. Include a deliberately non-default initial snapshot and changing channels.Player release and playback epoch share one acknowledged transition. No elapsed-time catch-up, acquisition blend, relocation or delayed default-state frame. Native replay time owns motion/state; countdown never advances it.
3 · Parked completion and other endingsA short taxi-to-parking take with hot final state; hold its endpoint 60 seconds. Also exercise a valid airborne ending, moving-ground ending and invalid/truncated input. Record terrain/gear behavior and final state retention, engine sound and object count.Only a validated stationary, grounded hot endpoint qualifies for parked completion; no invented taxi-in or engine restart. Non-parking behavior needs user agreement. Endpoint eligibility and numerical tolerances need measured acceptance criteria.
4 · Restart, pause and failureRestart the mission from countdown, playing, parked and failed states. Inject readiness mismatch, clock/state failure and missing aircraft. Pause/resume during countdown and playback. Inspect controller restoration, effects, callbacks, flags and object count.One fresh epoch, one initial snapshot, no stale callbacks, duplicated aircraft, smoke/audio residue or native hooks from the old run. Failure stops playback, releases recorder-owned player hold and cleans owned objects; the mission can continue. Full mission restart is the V1 replay route.
5 · Stand-in, conditionalRun only if experiment 1 fails with the real object. Repeat first-visible, countdown and release observations, including sound/smoke and contact, with frame-by-frame handoff evidence.Fallback must satisfy the same contract; otherwise staging remains blocked. Do not quietly weaken the visibility requirement.

Across these experiments, capture every supported exterior/engine channel at the same initial/final timestamp and document unavailable channels. Recheck speed-brake argument 21 after native animation: the accepted visual baseline had a maximum normalized residual of 0.010002; that is historical evidence, not an agreed tolerance. Include initial hold, changing brake and endpoint hold. Ground lighting, contact, collision and wake retain their separate acceptance gates.

The accepted implementation currently late-activates the lead at release and removes it at completion. Native initialization starts the tape automatically; early activation alone therefore cannot provide a held aircraft. Completion restores native hooks, so simply removing the destruction call cannot provide parked completion. A stand-in must also respect the current one-custom-playback-object ownership guard.
